## Installation & usage

<InstallTabs name="capsule-player" noImport />

Use a capsule player as a compact now-playing bar: at the foot of a music app, over a video timeline, in the corner of a podcast page. It shows the cover, the title and artist, a thin progress rail you can seek, the time played, and the transport buttons.

The player has no clock of its own. Pass it the playhead and the length from your audio or video element, and act on its callbacks.

```tsx
import { CapsulePlayer } from '@youj/react'

<CapsulePlayer
  title="Low Tide"
  subtitle="Mara Quell"
  artwork={<img src={cover} alt="" />}
  playing={playing}
  onPlayingChange={(next) => (next ? audio.play() : audio.pause())}
  position={time}
  duration={audio.duration}
  onSeek={(seconds) => (audio.currentTime = seconds)}
  onPrevious={previousTrack}
  onNext={nextTrack}
/>
```

Without `onSeek` the rail only shows progress. Without `onPrevious` or `onNext`, that button is disabled.

</Example>

<ReferenceRow title="Accessibility" summary="Keyboard">

### Accessibility

- The play button is named for what it does next: "Play", or "Pause" while playing.
- The rail is a slider named "Seek", spoken as the time and the length ("1:12 of 3:40"). The arrow keys move five seconds, and Home and End jump to the ends.
- The cover is decorative.
- The button names come from the labels (`play`, `pause`, `previousTrack`, `nextTrack`, `waveformSeek`, `waveformPosition`), which the language packs translate.

</ReferenceRow>
