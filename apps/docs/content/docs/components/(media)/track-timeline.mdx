A podcast edit on three tracks: Host, Guest and Music bed.

Moves and trims snap within 8px of another clip's edge, the playhead or either end; hold Alt to place freely, or pass `snap={false}`. The timeline opens with the whole of it in view (at least 8px a second); − and + (or Ctrl or ⌘ and the wheel) zoom about the playhead, and `zoom` controls it in pixels per second. Pass `playing` while your player runs: the level strips read each track at the playhead, and the lane turns the page as the playhead nears its end. The [Song Sketch](/docs/blocks/media#track-arranger) block puts it together with a transport, an inspector and simulated playback.

## Installation & usage

<InstallTabs name="track-timeline" noImport />

Use a track timeline for an edit made of parts on tracks: a podcast cut, a song sketch, a video's voice-over, a meeting's highlights. Each track is a lane in its chart color with its name, mute and solo keys and a level strip; each clip is a rounded bar holding the slice of the track's sound it plays, drawn from loudness samples you pass (it decodes no audio) and scaled by its gain. Drag a clip to move it, or its edges to trim it. Both snap to the other clips' edges and to the playhead, and a guide shows the edge they caught. A ruler of mono timecodes runs along the top, with the playhead on it. For a single recording's seek bar, use a [Waveform Scrubber](/docs/components/waveform-scrubber).

```tsx
import { TrackTimeline } from '@youj/react'

<TrackTimeline
  tracks={[
    { id: 'host', name: 'Host', caption: 'Mic 1', peaks: hostPeaks },
    { id: 'music', name: 'Music bed', peaks: musicPeaks, muted: true },
  ]}
  clips={clips}
  onClipsChange={setClips}
  onTrackChange={(id, change) => updateTrack(id, change)}
  duration={30}
  position={time}
  onPositionChange={setTime}
  value={selected}
  onValueChange={setSelected}
/>
```

`clips` is controlled: every move and trim reports the whole list through `onClipsChange`. A clip sits from `start` to `end` on its `track`, plays its track's source from `offset`, and scales its wave by `gain` in decibels. A track's `peaks` are its whole source as loudness from 0 to 1, one sample every `resolution` seconds (0.1). A clip never moves into its neighbor on the same track, never trims past its source, and stays at least half a second long. `formatClipTime(seconds, locale)` formats times the way the timeline speaks them (0:12.5).

<ReferenceRow title="Accessibility" summary="Keyboard, ARIA, focus">

### Accessibility

- The timeline is a group named by `label` (default "Timeline"), and each track a group named by the track. Every clip is a button and a tab stop, named by its name, range and track ("answer, 0:06.5 to 0:12.5, Guest") and described by a hint that explains the keys.
- Focusing a clip selects it (`aria-pressed`). Arrow keys move it by `step` (half a second), Shift ten times as far, stopping at the first snap point on the way; the new range is announced. Escape lets go of it.
- The selected clip's edges come next in the tab order, as sliders named "Start of answer" and "End of answer" that trim it from the keyboard.
- The playhead is a slider on the ruler, spoken as the time and the length ("0:09.0 of 0:30.0"). Mute and solo are toggle buttons named with their track ("Mute Guest").
- Time runs left to right in every script, like a tape, so the lane keeps its direction under RTL, the arrow keys included; the heads and their text follow the page.
- With sound on, catching a snap point while dragging plays `tick`.
- Its words come from the labels (`trackTimeline`, `trackTimelineHint`, `trackTimelineClip`, `trackTimelineRange`, `trackTimelineClipStart`, `trackTimelineClipEnd`, `trackTimelineMute`, `trackTimelineSolo`, `trackTimelinePlayhead`, `zoomIn`, `zoomOut`), translated by the language packs.

</ReferenceRow>
