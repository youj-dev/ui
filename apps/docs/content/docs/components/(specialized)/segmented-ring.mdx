## Installation & usage

<InstallTabs name="segmented-ring" noImport />

Use an segmented ring for anything that runs in timed intervals: HIIT and Tabata, a pomodoro day, a breathing exercise. Each interval is an arc as long as it lasts, round the ring clockwise from the top inside a ruler of ten-second ticks. Work and rest take their own chart colors, and warm-up and cool-down share a third: intervals behind you are lit, the one running fills with a bright head at its edge, and the ones ahead wait in a dim wash of their color, so the shape of the whole session reads at a glance. The center holds the round in big mono, the phase and the time left in it. For a day of scheduled jobs, use a [Day Ring](/docs/components/day-ring).

```tsx
import { SegmentedRing, type SegmentedRingInterval } from '@youj/react'

const intervals: SegmentedRingInterval[] = [
  { kind: 'warmup', duration: 60 },
  { kind: 'work', duration: 40, label: 'Kettlebell swings' },
  { kind: 'rest', duration: 20 },
  { kind: 'work', duration: 40, label: 'Goblet squats' },
]

<SegmentedRing intervals={intervals} elapsed={seconds} />
```

The ring keeps no clock of its own: `elapsed` is the seconds since the session started, so run your own timer and pause, skip or rewind it by changing `elapsed`. `duration` is in seconds, `kind` picks the phase and its color (or give an interval its own `tone`), and `label` names what's done in it, spoken as the interval starts. The round counts work intervals: a warm-up leads into round 1, and a rest belongs to the round before it.

<ReferenceRow title="Accessibility" summary="announcements">

### Accessibility

- The ring is a group named by `label` (default: the labels' "Interval timer"), with a summary of the round, the phase and the time left ("Round 3 of 8. Work, 0:24 left").
- As an interval starts, a polite live region announces its phase, its length and what follows ("Rest, 0:20. Next: Goblet squats."), and the end is announced too.
- Sound: the notify cue plays as each interval starts, a tick in each of its last three seconds, and success at the end, behind `sound` (on by default). A rewind stays quiet.
- Its words come from the labels (`intervalTimer`, `intervalWork`, `intervalRest`, `intervalWarmup`, `intervalCooldown`, `intervalRound`, `intervalRoundOf`, `intervalLeft`, `intervalStarted`, `intervalFinished`, `intervalNext`), translated by the language packs.
- A dial turns clockwise in every script, so the ring keeps its direction under RTL; the center's text follows the page.

</ReferenceRow>
