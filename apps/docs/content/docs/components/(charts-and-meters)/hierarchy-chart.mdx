A company's org chart. Select a person to light their reporting line, and close a branch with the chip on a card's foot to fold it into a count ("+3").

The chart opens fitted to its canvas, but never below 60%, so names stay legible; a bigger chart opens on its root and pans instead. Drag anywhere to pan (a click that doesn't move still picks a node), use the wheel, or one finger across; zoom with Ctrl or ⌘ and the wheel, a pinch, the corner buttons or + and −, and press 0 to fit the whole chart.

## Installation & usage

<InstallTabs name="hierarchy-chart" noImport />

Use a hierarchy chart when the shape of a tree is the point: who reports to whom, which teams sit under which group, how a product breaks down. For a tree people browse and pick from (files, settings, categories), use a [Tree View](/docs/components/tree-view); for steps that depend on each other, a [Flow Graph](/docs/components/flow-graph).

```tsx
import { HierarchyChart } from '@youj/react'

<HierarchyChart
  nodes={{
    id: 'maya',
    name: 'Maya Lindqvist',
    role: 'CEO',
    children: [
      { id: 'jonas', name: 'Jonas Okafor', role: 'VP Engineering', badge: 'Hiring', children: [/* … */] },
      { id: 'elena', name: 'Elena Petrova', role: 'VP Product' },
    ],
  }}
  selected={selected}
  onSelectedChange={setSelected}
/>
```

`nodes` is one root or an array of them, each `{ id, name, role?, avatar?, badge?, children? }`; the default card shows the avatar (or the name's initials), the name, the role and the badge. Pass your own type that extends `HierarchyNode` and a `renderNode(node, state)` to draw the card yourself; `state` says its depth, whether it's selected, on the selected path, a branch and open, and how many nodes sit below it. Every card has one size, `nodeSize` (224 × 64 by default).

The layout is a tidy tree (Reingold–Tilford, in Walker's linear form): every parent is centered over its children, subtrees pack as close as the gaps allow without overlapping, and identical subtrees are drawn identically. It handles a few hundred nodes at once; `layoutHierarchy(nodes, options)` returns the same layout for drawing your own.

Branches are all open by default; `defaultExpanded` or `expanded` with `onExpandedChange` take the open ids. To find someone, hold a `ref`: `ref.current.focusNode(id)` opens the branches above them, focuses their card and centers it, and `ref.current.fit()` fits the chart.

<ReferenceRow title="Accessibility" summary="Keyboard, ARIA">

### Accessibility

- The chart is a WAI-ARIA `tree` named by `label` (default "Hierarchy chart") and described by a hint that explains the keys. Each card is a `treeitem` with `aria-level`, `aria-setsize`, `aria-posinset`, `aria-selected` and, on a branch, `aria-expanded`. It's named by the node's `name` and described by its role, its badge and, when closed, how many it hides ("3 hidden below").
- One Tab stop. ↑ and ↓ move through the nodes in reading order, → opens a branch or steps into it, ← closes it or steps out to its parent (mirrored under RTL), Home and End go to the first and last node, and * opens every sibling branch. Enter opens or closes a branch, Space selects. +, − and 0 zoom and fit.
- Focus from the keyboard brings its node into view, and closing a branch that holds the focus hands it to the branch.
- The open/close chip is a pointer shortcut for Enter, hidden from assistive technology; selection is shown by a border and a ring as well as the path's color.
- With sound on, selecting plays `select`, opening a branch `open` and closing it `close`.
- Its words come from the labels (`hierarchyChart`, `hierarchyChartHint`, `hierarchyChartHidden`, `hierarchyChartExpand`, `hierarchyChartCollapse`, `zoomIn`, `zoomOut`, `zoomToFit`), translated by the language packs.

</ReferenceRow>
