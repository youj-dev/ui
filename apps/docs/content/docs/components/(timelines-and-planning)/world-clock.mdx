## Installation & usage

<InstallTabs name="world-clock" noImport />

Use a world clock wherever people in different places have to meet: a team page, a meeting planner, a support rota, a booking that crosses borders. Each row is a zone or a person, with its name, its offset from UTC and from the first row, the time there with a sun or a moon, and a strip of the day. For one place's day as a ring, use [Day Ring](/docs/components/day-ring); for shifts across a week, [Time Lanes](/docs/components/time-lanes).

```tsx
import { WorldClock, type WorldClockZone } from '@youj/react'

const zones: WorldClockZone[] = [
  { timeZone: 'Europe/London', label: 'Ada · London' },
  { timeZone: 'America/New_York', label: 'Grace · New York' },
  { timeZone: 'Asia/Singapore', label: 'Lin · Singapore', workingHours: [8.5, 17.5] },
]

const [value, setValue] = useState<Date | null>(null)

<WorldClock zones={zones} value={value} onValueChange={setValue} step={30} />
```

A zone is an IANA `timeZone`, a `label`, and its `workingHours` as local hours from 0 to 24 (`[9, 17]` by default; `[9, 17.5]` ends at 17:30, and `[22, 6]` is a night shift). The waking hours outside work run from 07:00 to 22:00, and the rest is night.

The rows share one time axis: the first zone's day, midnight to midnight. On the days its clocks change that day is 23 or 25 hours long, and every offset is read from each zone's own clock at each moment, so the gap between two zones follows their changes too. Where a zone's date turns over, a line marks it and the cell names the new day.

The cursor is the chosen moment. With no `value` it follows the clock (`now`, by default the device's, ticking every second once the page is live); drag it, click a strip, or use the keys, and it snaps to `step` minutes (15 by default). `onValueChange` gets a `Date`, or `null` when Now is pressed. Moving past either end of the day moves the axis to the next or the previous one. Times read in 12 or 24 hours by the locale, unless `hourCycle` says otherwise; `compact` keeps one line per zone and drops the legend.

<ReferenceRow title="Accessibility" summary="Keyboard, announcements">

### Accessibility

- The whole is a group named by `label` (default: the labels' "World clock"). Each zone is a list item that reads its name, then its time, day, offset and whether it's day or night ("05:00, Tue 13 Jan, GMT-5, Night"); the strips are hidden from screen readers.
- The cursor's handle is a slider named "Proposed time", described by how to move it, and its value reads the moment in the first zone ("Tue 13 Jan, 10:00 in London").
- Left and right move it a step (mirrored in RTL), as do up and down; Shift with an arrow, or Page Up and Page Down, an hour; Home and End go to the ends of the day. Now returns to the clock and puts focus back on the handle.
- After every change a polite live region says the moment in every zone: "10:00 in London is 05:00 in New York and 18:00 in Singapore", with "tomorrow" or "yesterday" where a zone is on another day.
- Its words come from the labels (`worldClock`, `worldClockCursor`, `worldClockHint`, `worldClockTime`, `worldClockSummary`, `worldClockOverlap`, `worldClockNoOverlap`, `worldClockWorkingHours`, `worldClockOutsideHours`, `worldClockNight`, `worldClockDaytime`, `worldClockEveryone` and `now`), translated by the language packs; dates, offsets and "tomorrow" come from Intl in the locale.

</ReferenceRow>
