A grid tells the reader they're looking at a drawing: something measured, with parts in their places. Put one behind an isometric scene, a hero figure, or an empty state's illustration. Use one grid per surface, and keep it faint: it's paper, not content.

## Installation

<InstallTabs name="grid-backdrops" imports="DotGrid, LineGrid, IsoGrid" />

## Usage

Each backdrop fills its nearest positioned ancestor and paints behind the content, like the [background components](/docs/components/mesh-gradient), so give the container `relative isolate`.

```tsx
import { IsoScene, IsoStack, LineGrid } from '@youj/illustrations'

export function Figure() {
  return (
    <figure className="relative isolate rounded-xl border border-line bg-surface p-8">
      <LineGrid gap={20} />
      <IsoScene explode>…</IsoScene>
    </figure>
  )
}
```

- **`DotGrid`**: dots on a square grid, `gap` px apart, of radius `size`.
- **`LineGrid`**: hairlines `gap` px apart, a stronger line every `major` cells, and a small registration cross where two strong lines meet. `major={0}` leaves only the fine lines.
- **`IsoGrid`**: the isometric rhombi of a scene's floor, one grid unit every `gap` px, for a backdrop that shares the drawing's angles.

## Examples

<Examples>

<Example id="fading" label="Fading">

### Fading

`fade` sets how a backdrop meets the edge of its container. `radial` (the default) fades out from the middle, so the paper is densest behind the figure. `edges` fades a band along each side and keeps the middle even, for a wide panel. `none` runs it to the edge, for a container that clips it with its own border.

</Example>

<Example id="a-scenes-floor" label="A scene's floor">

### A scene's floor

`IsoFloor` goes inside an [IsoScene](/docs/illustrations/draw-your-own#the-scene): the scene's own grid on the floor, in its units, from the origin to `w` by `d`, every `step` units. Shapes placed on whole numbers stand on its lines. Draw it first, so it stays under the shapes, as lines or, with `kind="dots"`, as dots at the crossings.

</Example>

<Example id="color" label="Color">

### Color

The lines and dots are the ink at a low strength over transparent, so they read on a surface, a raised panel or the canvas, in either scheme and every preset, and a theme change repaints them with no JavaScript.

</Example>

<ReferenceRow title="Accessibility" summary="ARIA">

### Accessibility

- Backdrops are decorative: each is `aria-hidden` and ignores pointer events.
- They sit at a low strength, but check the contrast of any text you set over the densest part of a grid.
